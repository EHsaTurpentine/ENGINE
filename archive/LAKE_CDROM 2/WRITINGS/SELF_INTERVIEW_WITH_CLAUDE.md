Claude, I have a series of images I want to use for a multimodal memoir project. The images were produced in a digital coloring book app on my iPad called Lake. I had a serious health incident 4 years ago which required 2 emergency surgeries on June 27, 2022 to save my life and 4 surgeries over about 10 months total; the fourth surgery was successfully completed in April of 2023. As I recovered from the first 2 surgeries in the hospital, I began using Lake for a number of reasons; one of those reasons was to document the recovery of my fine motor skills; reading and writing and even thinking were all things I had to relearn.

As I continued to make myself use Lake during that time in the hospital, I knew I was amassing material I'd use later. It wasn't until well after I got out of the hospital for good I realized what the images were in one regard was an actual trauma journal, if for no other reason than THESE ARE THE VERY IMAGES I MADE when I was actively experiencing an actual trauma.

I've already written about BUNNY COLVIN in other pieces you've used in other projects we've made, but the key thing about him is he came to me while I was in the hospital and I know the exact date I made that image of him with the diamond map and green lizard and yellow daffodils: July 22, 2022. I'd been released on July 20 but my blood pressure started spiking and so I went back to the hospital 2 days later and was, in a single word, FURIOUS about going back. But I had no choice and no one was to blame and I had to deal with all of that and it was that very first night, July 22, when I opened up the Lake app and created that first image of him.

This project isn't about him, though. What I want to do is give you these images and then roleplay with me as a talking/typing therapist. I want you to ask me at least 5 different questions about any or all of the images I created in Lake, and then I'll use our conversation in a new text-image piece. The deliverable in this is a Markdown document that reads like an actual interview transcription.

Something to inform our conversation (but not necessarily important to explicitly discuss in our conversation) is this: I had a therapist who worked magic for me before and also during the pandemic; unfortunately, he died suddenly right after the pandemic was over, no doubt the result of how he handled his practice: he was always available to his patients and put himself at remarkable risk to serve us. Eric Bergreen was his name and his obituary is probably still somewhere on the web; he had a practice in Oregon City, OR and I was his patient off and on for the better part of a decade.
