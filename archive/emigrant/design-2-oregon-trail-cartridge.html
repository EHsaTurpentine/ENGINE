You have reached the eastern border of the Idaho Territory. Your journey is not over — not by half. Ahead lie river crossings, cutoffs, and roads with names like Kelton, Goodale, and Hudspeth, first walked by fur trappers and drawn onto U.S. Geological Survey quadrangles more than a century later.

This page is built from a real field survey: BLM Idaho Cultural Resource Series No. 1, published January 1993 — 244 pages, 121 maps, one long argument for looking down at the ground instead of just driving over it.

WAYPOINT — SMITH’S TRADING POST

“Peg Leg” Smith set up shop on the Bear River in 1848, trading with wagon trains passing through. The post is long gone — two seasons and it folded — but a highway marker on U.S. 30 still flags the spot today.
